## Abstract (extracted from the PDF)

> This paper empirically tests how Economic Policy Uncertainty (EPU) and market volatility (India VIX) affect three systemically important Indian financial markets the equity market (Nifty 50), the foreign exchange market (USD/INR), and the commodity market (Brent crude oil) using 121 monthly observations between March 2016 and March 2026. The study uses an eight-step econometric model with ARDL bounds testing, GARCH(1,1), EGARCH(1,1), and Granger causality analysis to conclude that all the variables are I(0), there is no long-run cointegration between any two markets and that the impacts of uncertainty are short-term in nature. India VIX Granger-causes Nifty 50 returns (F=6.79, p=0.010) and EPU Granger-causes crude oil returns (F=4.22, p=0.042). The volatility persistence is also found to be much higher across the different asset classes (USD/INR: 0.9932; crude oil: 0.6403; Nifty 50: 0.4644). The results are a challenge to the assumption of homogeneous transmission of uncertainty and provide implications that can be taken to practice in portfolio management, FX hedging, energy planning, and regulatory stress-testing.
