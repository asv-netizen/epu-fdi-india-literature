## Abstract

> Abstract Private investment is a central determinant of capital accumulation, productive capacity, employment creation, and long-run economic growth. This study examines the relationship between economic policy uncertainty (EPU) and private investment in India within a data-consistent dynamic time-series framework. The preferred dependent variable is gross fixed capital formation of private non-financial corporations, while real GDP, the real interest rate, private-sector credit, and public investment are considered as macroeconomic controls. The India EPU index is converted from monthly observations to financial-year averages so that it is aligned with India's National Accounts. Particular attention is given to the compatibility of the historical 2011–12-base national-accounts series and the newly introduced 2022–23-base system. Verified observations for FY2011–12 to FY2023–24 show substantial variation in both private investment and policy uncertainty, including pronounced uncertainty episodes during major macroeconomic shocks. However, the currently verified common annual sample is too short for a credible six-variable ARDL, bounds-test, ECM, and causality system. Accordingly, the present version does not report invented econometric statistics. Instead, it documents the verified dataset, its construction, descriptive properties, theoretical mechanism, and a reproducible estimation protocol. The paper's main methodological contribution is to demonstrate how policy-uncertainty research on Indian aggregate investment should address frequency alignment, national-account revisions, variable definitions, and small-sample identification before dynamic coefficients are interpreted.
