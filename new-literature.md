## 0. Gap check: does an India NARDL EPU–FDI paper exist?

**Result: I found none, but the gap statement needs one correction.**

1. **There is a second India EPU–FDI paper that is not in your folder:** Sinha & Ghosh (2021), entry 1 below. So "Khan & Qamar is the only India EPU–FDI study" is not true. Both papers are linear, so the asymmetry gap still holds.
2. **Asymmetric (NARDL-type) work on India does exist, but not for FDI.** It covers aggregate investment (Adil & Roy 2024) and the stock market (Simran & Sharma 2024). Your gap statement should name these and say why they are different: investment is not FDI, and stock prices are not FDI.
3. **Suggested gap wording:** "Existing India evidence on EPU and FDI (Sinha & Ghosh 2021; Khan & Qamar 2023) is linear and annual or short-sample. Asymmetric methods have been applied in India to aggregate investment (Adil & Roy 2024) and equity markets (Simran & Sharma 2024), but not to FDI. No study estimates horizon-resolved or regime-dependent EPU–FDI effects for India."
4. **Limits of this check.** I searched the open web, RePEc/IDEAS, publisher pages and DOAJ. I did not have Scopus, Web of Science or Google Scholar full-text access. Before you submit, run this query in Scopus and Google Scholar: `("policy uncertainty" OR EPU) AND (FDI OR "foreign direct investment") AND India AND (NARDL OR asymmetric OR nonlinear OR "local projection" OR quantile)`. Also check Indian journals that are poorly indexed: *Indian Economic Review*, *Margin*, *Journal of Quantitative Economics*, *Global Business Review*, *Foreign Trade Review*, *South Asian Journal of Macroeconomics and Public Finance*.

## A. India: EPU, investment and FDI (Chapters 2, 3 and the gap statement)

**1. Sinha, A. & Ghosh, T. (2021).** "Impact of Economic Policy Uncertainty on FDI Inflows: Evidence from India." In W. A. Barnett & B. S. Sergi (eds.), *Recent Developments in Asian Economics*, International Symposia in Economic Theory and Econometrics, Vol. 28, pp. 157–167. Emerald. DOI: [10.1108/S1571-038620210000028009](https://doi.org/10.1108/S1571-038620210000028009)
- **Adds:** This is the missing India EPU–FDI paper. It finds that EPU has a **larger effect in the long run than in the short run**, which supports your H3 (horizon) and gives you a direct prior to test against.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 2 (India literature) and the gap statement. Cite it next to Khan & Qamar.
- **Confidence:** Med. The keywords say "autoregressive distributed lag", so it is very likely a linear ARDL. The sample period and frequency are behind the paywall; get the chapter through your library.

**2. Adil, M. H. & Roy, A. (2024).** "Asymmetric effects of uncertainty on investment: Empirical evidence from India." *Journal of Economic Asymmetries*, 29, e00359. DOI: [10.1016/j.jeca.2024.e00359](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeca.2024.e00359)
- **Adds:** This is the closest neighbour to your thesis. It finds asymmetric cointegration plus short-run, long-run and adjustment asymmetries between uncertainty and **aggregate investment** in India, and asymmetric Granger causality. It shows that the asymmetry idea works in Indian data. Your contribution is to move from total investment to FDI and to add horizon and regime analysis.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 2 and the gap statement; also Ch. 5 as a benchmark for the size of the asymmetry.
- **Confidence:** Med. The abstract is checked. The exact uncertainty measure and sample are behind the paywall.

**3. Bhagat, S., Ghosh, P. & Rangan, S. (2016).** "Economic Policy Uncertainty and Growth in India." *Economic and Political Weekly*, 51(35), 72ff. Working-paper version: IIM Bangalore WP No. 407 (2013), [PDF](https://repository.iimb.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/7903/1/WP_IIMB_407.pdf)
- **Adds:** This is the construction paper for the India EPU index. The index is two-thirds newspaper coverage from 7 Indian papers and one-third forecaster disagreement on inflation and the budget balance. A VAR(6) shows that a 1 s.d. EPU shock lowers **fixed investment by up to 0.53%, with the effect lasting about 12 months**. You need this to describe exactly what `INDEPUINDXM` measures.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 3 (measurement); Ch. 2 (investment channel).
- **Confidence:** High for the WP content and the EPW citation. **Mem:** I believe policyuncertainty.com's India series is built on this work and that its current version may be news-only; check the India data notes on policyuncertainty.com.

**4. Priyaranjan, N. & Pratap, B. (2020).** "Macroeconomic Effects of Uncertainty: A Big Data Analysis for India." RBI Working Paper Series No. 04/2020. [RBI page](https://www.rbi.org.in/scripts/PublicationsView.aspx?Id=19431)
- **Adds:** This paper builds three alternative India uncertainty indices: a newspaper EPU index from ET, BusinessLine and FE; a news-sentiment index; and a Google Trends index. It combines them by PCA. It then uses **local projections**: investment falls by about 2% for up to four quarters. It gives you (a) a robustness EPU measure built in India and (b) an India local-projection precedent for your horizon chapter.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 3 (alternative measure); Ch. 6 (LP precedent).
- **Confidence:** High.

**5. Simran & Sharma, A. K. (2024).** "Asymmetric nexus between economic policy uncertainty and the Indian stock market: Evidence using NARDL approach." *Quarterly Review of Economics and Finance*, 93, 91–101. DOI: [10.1016/j.qref.2023.11.006](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.qref.2023.11.006)
- **Adds:** This is an India NARDL paper using India EPU, 2003–2022, with a pre-COVID subsample. It is by the same authors as the crypto paper in your folder, but it is a different paper. Its key result is that **falling EPU raises stock prices more than rising EPU lowers them**. That is the opposite direction to your H2. It is useful because it shows the asymmetry direction is an empirical question, and because it shows NARDL with India EPU has passed review.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 5 (asymmetry; competing hypotheses); Ch. 2.
- **Confidence:** High.

**6. Government of India, Ministry of Finance (2025).** *Economic Survey 2024–25*, Ch. 3, "External Sector: Getting FDI Right." [PDF](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/budget2025-26/economicsurvey/doc/eschapter/echap03.pdf)
- **Adds:** This is the official account of the recent FDI slowdown. Gross FDI rose about 18% (Apr–Nov 2024), but **net FDI collapsed to USD 0.48 bn** against USD 8.5 bn a year earlier, because repatriation reached USD 44.5 bn in FY24. It also names tax and regulatory certainty as a policy issue. This strongly supports using **gross inflows**, not net FDI, as your dependent variable, and it motivates a post-2022 regime.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 1 (motivation); Ch. 3 (gross vs net; stylised facts); Ch. 7 (latest regime).
- **Confidence:** High.

**7. Sherry, A. M. & Maheshwari, S. (2026).** "India's Inward and Outward Investment Flows: Concentration, Imbalances, and Policy Strategy." *Economic and Political Weekly*, 61(38). [EPW](https://www.epw.in/journal/2026/38/special-articles/indias-inward-and-outward-investment-flows.html)
- **Adds:** This paper shows growing concentration in India's FDI by source country, sector and state. Concentration matters for your "lumpy FDI" risk and for any source-country (global EPU) argument.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 3 (stylised facts); Ch. 9 (policy).
- **Confidence:** Med. Only the abstract is open.

## B. Asymmetric and heterogeneous EPU–FDI evidence elsewhere (Chapter 2, Chapters 5 and 8)

**8. Elroukh, A. W., Al-Hasani, I. & Gani, A. (2025).** "The asymmetric impact of global economic policy uncertainty on FDI in the GCC countries." *Asian Economic and Financial Review*, 15(11), 1748–1762. DOI: [10.55493/5002.v15i11.5664](https://doi.org/10.55493/5002.v15i11.5664)
- **Adds:** This is a panel NARDL of **global** EPU on FDI for 6 GCC economies, 1997–2023. Both rises and falls in EPU lower FDI, which the authors call "persistent investor caution". That pattern is a hysteresis-like result. It is a direct emerging-market NARDL precedent and a check on your global-EPU question.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 2; Ch. 5; Ch. 8 (global vs domestic).
- **Confidence:** High. The journal is lower-tier, so cite it as supporting evidence, not as a central reference.

**9. Canh, N. P., Binh, N. T., Thanh, S. D. & Schinckus, C. (2020).** "Determinants of foreign direct investment inflows: The role of economic policy uncertainty." *International Economics*, 161, 159–172. [RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/cii/cepiie/2020-q1-161-12.html)
- **Adds:** This is a 21-country panel, 2003–2013. **Domestic EPU growth lowers FDI, while world uncertainty (WUI) raises it** once it is conditioned on domestic EPU. That is the opposite of Jardet et al.'s emphasis, so together the two papers set up your "domestic vs global" question as a real contest.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 2; Ch. 8.
- **Confidence:** High for the content. The DOI is not on RePEc; take it from the ScienceDirect page (PII S211070171930040X).

**10. Gao, H., Chen, W., Li, J. & Zhou, T. (2024).** "Economic policy uncertainty and foreign direct investment: Evidence from China." *Structural Change and Economic Dynamics*, 70, 178–191. DOI: [10.1016/j.strueco.2024.01.014](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.strueco.2024.01.014)
- **Adds:** This is a 264-city China panel, 2005–2014, with an open-economy model. **FDI becomes more sensitive to EPU after the 2008 crisis.** That is direct evidence that the EPU–FDI relationship changes over time, which supports your evolution hypothesis (H4) in a large emerging market.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 2; Ch. 7.
- **Confidence:** High.

**11. Phan, N. H., Bui, V. D. & Nguyen, L. T. Q. (2023).** "How economic policy uncertainty affects foreign direct investment inflows: Implications for the post-Covid-19 pandemic period." *International Journal of Social Economics*, 51(2), 282–296. DOI: [10.1108/IJSE-02-2023-0114](https://doi.org/10.1108/IJSE-02-2023-0114)
- **Adds:** This study uses UNCTAD data for 213 countries, 2003–2020, and splits FDI into **greenfield and cross-border M&A**. EPU effects differ by type: COVID-era uncertainty went with **higher M&A only**. This is the best cross-country precedent for your greenfield vs acquisition test.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 8 (composition).
- **Confidence:** High.

**12. Tran, T. M. T. & To, N. L. (2025).** "Spillover Effects of Foreign Policy Uncertainty and their Impact on FDI Inflows in Asia–Pacific: A Panel Quantile Approach." *ECONOMICS*, 13(4), 293–312. DOI: [10.2478/eoik-2025-0097](https://doi.org/10.2478/eoik-2025-0097)
- **Adds:** This paper covers 21 Asia-Pacific economies, 1993–2023, and uses Diebold–Yilmaz spillovers plus quantile regression. Foreign EPU **lowers FDI in low-FDI economies (−0.51%) but raises it in high-FDI economies (+0.52%)**, a "flight to safety" effect. It offers a mechanism for a positive sign (compare Marozva & Magwedere) and a reason to treat global EPU separately.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 2; Ch. 8.
- **Confidence:** High.

**13. Williams, M., Abu Alrub, A. & Aga, M. (2022).** "Ecological footprint, economic uncertainty and foreign direct investment in South Africa: Evidence from asymmetric cointegration and dynamic multipliers in a nonlinear ARDL approach." *SAGE Open*, 12(2). DOI: [10.1177/21582440221094607](https://doi.org/10.1177/21582440221094607)
- **Adds:** This is a **monthly** single-country NARDL on FDI (1996–2017) with dynamic multipliers. Use it only as a method template showing that monthly FDI NARDL with multipliers is publishable. Its focus is environmental, not EPU.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 5 (methods precedent).
- **Confidence:** High. Its relevance is limited.

## C. Horizon methods: local projections and their pitfalls (Chapter 6)

**14. Jordà, Ò. (2005).** "Estimation and Inference of Impulse Responses by Local Projections." *American Economic Review*, 95(1), 161–182. DOI: [10.1257/0002828053828518](https://doi.org/10.1257/0002828053828518)
- **Adds:** This is the founding paper for your horizon step and must be cited.
- **Confidence:** Mem.

**15. Cezar, R., Gigout, T. & Tripier, F. (2020).** "Cross-border Investments and Uncertainty: Firm-level Evidence." Banque de France WP No. 766. [Link](https://publications.banque-france.fr/en/cross-border-investments-and-uncertainty-firm-level-evidence)
- **Adds:** This is **Jordà local projections applied directly to uncertainty and FDI**, using French MNE outward FDI, 2000–2015. Uncertainty delays FDI. Low-performing firms cut FDI durably, with no recovery, while high performers increase it. The no-recovery result is a firm-level version of hysteresis and supports your H2 and H3.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 6 (LP precedent); Ch. 2 (hysteresis evidence).
- **Confidence:** High.

**16. Ahir, H., Bettarelli, L., Furceri, D., Frangiamore, F., Ostry, J. D. & Scianna, F. (2026).** "Trade Restrictions, Trade Policy Uncertainty and FDI Flows." CEPR DP 21524; also Bruegel WP 12/26; summary in SUERF Policy Brief No. 1537 (Aug 2026). [SUERF brief](https://www.suerf.org/publications/suerf-policy-notes-and-briefs/beyond-trade-barriers-how-trade-policy-uncertainty-weakens-foreign-direct-investment/)
- **Adds:** This study uses gravity-based **local projections over 5 years** of bilateral FDI into 35 OECD hosts. A 1 s.d. trade-policy-uncertainty shock lowers FDI by about 40% on impact and about 50% after one year, and the effect is still significant at five years. It is very recent, and its horizon profile is the shape your H3 predicts.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 6; Ch. 2; also supports a post-2018 trade-policy regime in H4.
- **Confidence:** High for the brief. I did not read the DP itself.

**17. Kilian, L. & Vigfusson, R. J. (2011).** "Are the responses of the U.S. economy asymmetric in energy price increases and decreases?" *Quantitative Economics*, 2(3), 419–453. DOI: [10.3982/QE99](https://doi.org/10.3982/QE99)
- **Adds:** This is the standard critique of **censored, sign-split regressors**, the same construction as your EPU⁺ and EPU⁻ and your S⁺ and S⁻ shocks. It argues that slope tests can be misleading and that asymmetry should be tested on **impulse responses to shocks of a given size**. Citing it, and running its test, protects the asymmetric-LP chapter in a viva.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 5 and Ch. 6 (method caveats); Ch. 9 risks table.
- **Confidence:** High.

**18. Gonçalves, S., Herrera, A. M., Kilian, L. & Pesavento, E. (2024).** "State-dependent local projections." *Journal of Econometrics*, 244(2), 105702. DOI: [10.1016/j.jeconom.2024.105702](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeconom.2024.105702)
- **Adds:** This paper shows that state-dependent LPs recover the true response only when the state is **exogenous**. Otherwise, responses can be off by up to 82%. This matters if you interact LPs with regimes: use Bai–Perron dates or exogenous regime dummies, not states that EPU itself drives.
- **Slots into:** Ch. 6; Ch. 7.
- **Confidence:** High.

**19. Shin, Y., Yu, B. & Greenwood-Nimmo, M. (2014).** "Modelling Asymmetric Cointegration and Dynamic Multipliers in a Nonlinear ARDL Framework." In R. Sickles & W. Horrace (eds.), *Festschrift in Honor of Peter Schmidt*, Springer, 281–314. DOI: [10.1007/978-1-4899-8008-3_9](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-1-4899-8008-3_9)
- **Adds:** This is the original NARDL paper. You cite it in your design but it is not in the folder. Get the PDF.
- **Confidence:** High for the DOI; the page range is Mem.

## D. Foundational theory you cite but do not yet hold (Chapter 2)

**20. Bernanke, B. S. (1983).** "Irreversibility, Uncertainty, and Cyclical Investment." *Quarterly Journal of Economics*, 98(1), 85–106. DOI: [10.2307/1885568](https://doi.org/10.2307/1885568). It is the source of the "bad news principle": only the downside matters for the decision to wait. That is a theory-based reason for asymmetry. **Mem.**

**21. Dixit, A. (1989).** "Entry and Exit Decisions under Uncertainty." *Journal of Political Economy*, 97(3), 620–638. DOI: [10.1086/261619](https://doi.org/10.1086/261619). It gives the band of inaction and hysteresis, which is your H2 mechanism. Pair it with **Baldwin, R. & Krugman, P. (1989)**, "Persistent Trade Effects of Large Exchange Rate Shocks," *QJE*, 104(4), 635–654, DOI: [10.2307/2937860](https://doi.org/10.2307/2937860). **Mem.**

**22. Dixit, A. K. & Pindyck, R. S. (1994).** *Investment under Uncertainty*. Princeton University Press. ISBN 978-0-691-03410-2. Cite Chs. 2–3 and 7 for the option value of waiting, and the entry/exit chapters for hysteresis. **Mem.**

**23. Bloom, N. (2009).** "The Impact of Uncertainty Shocks." *Econometrica*, 77(3), 623–685. DOI: [10.3982/ECTA6248](https://doi.org/10.3982/ECTA6248). It shows a drop-and-rebound response with overshoot after uncertainty shocks. This gives a theory-based **horizon** prediction: the effect is fast at first, then partly recovers. It is a useful contrast to your H3. **Mem.**

**24. Growth-options / convexity cluster (Oi–Hartman–Abel):**
- Oi, W. Y. (1961). "The Desirability of Price Instability under Perfect Competition." *Econometrica*, 29(1), 58–64. DOI: [10.2307/1907687](https://doi.org/10.2307/1907687)
- Hartman, R. (1972). "The Effects of Price and Cost Uncertainty on Investment." *Journal of Economic Theory*, 5(2), 258–266. DOI: [10.1016/0022-0531(72)90105-6](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-0531(72)90105-6)
- Abel, A. B. (1983). "Optimal Investment under Uncertainty." *American Economic Review*, 73(1), 228–233.
- These papers are the theory behind a **positive** EPU–FDI sign (Marozva & Magwedere; Tran & To's flight to safety). **Mem.**

**25. Rodrik, D. (1991).** "Policy Uncertainty and Private Investment in Developing Countries." *Journal of Development Economics*, 36(2), 229–242. DOI: [10.1016/0304-3878(91)90034-S](https://doi.org/10.1016/0304-3878(91)90034-S). It shows that even a small chance of a **policy reversal** acts like a large tax on irreversible investment in developing countries. This is the cleanest theory link between *policy* uncertainty (not general uncertainty) and FDI in an emerging market. **Mem.**

**26. Gulen, H. & Ion, M. (2016).** "Policy Uncertainty and Corporate Investment." *Review of Financial Studies*, 29(3), 523–564. DOI: [10.1093/rfs/hhv050](https://doi.org/10.1093/rfs/hhv050). It shows that EPU effects are **stronger for firms with more irreversible investment**. This is the firm-level template for your greenfield (more irreversible) vs acquisition (more reversible) test. **Mem.**
