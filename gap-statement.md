# The gap statement — checked and corrected

This document exists because two claims in your draft research-questions text did not survive verification. Both matter for the gap, so both are corrected here.

---

## 1. H2 is written backwards

Your draft says the thesis tests H2 as `|β⁻| > |β⁺|`.

But your own definition says **EPU⁺ adds the monthly increases** in EPU. So:

- `|β⁺|` is the effect of a **rise** in EPU
- `|β⁻|` is the effect of a **fall** in EPU

"An increase in EPU causes more damage to FDI than the same decrease in EPU removes" is therefore **`|β⁺| > |β⁻|`**.

Write it that way. If you write `|β⁻| > |β⁺|`, your hypothesis says the opposite of your theory, and the hysteresis argument in Section 1 of your text contradicts the hypothesis in Section 2. A viva panel will find this in the first minute.

## 2. Khan & Qamar (2023) is not the only India EPU–FDI study

Your draft says it is. It is not.

There are **at least two** India EPU–FDI studies:

Sinha & Ghosh (2021) was **not in your folder**. It is now: `notes/I-india/2021-sinha-impact-of-economic-policy-uncertainty-on-fdi-inflows-evidence-from-ind.md`.

**Both are linear.** That is your actual gap, and it is a clean one:

> Existing India evidence on EPU and FDI is linear. It estimates one elasticity and assumes that a rise in uncertainty and a fall of the same size move FDI by equal and opposite amounts. Nothing in the Indian data has tested that assumption.

Do not write "no study exists". Write "no study tests the asymmetry".

## 3. Two India studies use asymmetric methods — but not on FDI

Your draft names these, and both are real. They must be cited, and you must say why FDI is different.

| Study | Outlet | Outcome variable | Method |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Adil & Roy (2024)** — *Asymmetric effects of uncertainty on investment: Empirical evidence from India* | Journal of Economic Asymmetries, DOI [10.1016/j.jeca.2024.e00359](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jeca.2024.e00359) | Total Indian investment | NARDL |
| **Simran & Sharma (2024)** — *Asymmetric nexus between economic policy uncertainty and the Indian stock market* | Journal of Economic Asymmetries | Indian equity market | NARDL |

Both are in `notes/I-india/`.

### Why FDI is not the same as either

This is the paragraph your gap statement needs. Write it explicitly.

**FDI versus total investment.** Total Indian investment is mostly domestic. It is financed from domestic savings and banking credit, and it responds to domestic demand and the domestic cost of capital. FDI crosses a border. The investor must decide whether to commit capital to India *or to another country*, and that decision is taken abroad, under foreign risk assessment, on a horizon of years. A shock that Indian firms absorb by postponing a machine purchase can send a foreign investor to Vietnam instead. The two respond to different margins.

**FDI versus share prices.** A share price re-prices in seconds. Portfolio investors reverse a position in a day. FDI is a factory: the money is committed before the plant is built and it cannot be withdrawn quickly. That is exactly the real-options asymmetry your thesis is built on — and it is why an equity market can show a *reversal* of the sign while FDI shows a *reinforcement*. If Simran & Sharma find the opposite direction to your H2, that is not a problem for your thesis. It is evidence that the asset matters, which is your claim.

> **Flag — read before you cite.** Your draft states that Simran & Sharma (2024) "found that a decrease in EPU has a larger effect than an increase", i.e. the opposite direction to your H2. That is a specific empirical claim. It is **not** something this review could confirm, because the full text is paywalled and the abstract was not retrievable. **Read the paper and confirm the direction before you put that sentence in a chapter.** If the direction is as you say, it strengthens your thesis rather than weakening it, for the reason in the paragraph above.

Your text calls RQ6 "an optional addition". After this search, it needs a warning label.

| Study | Outlet | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **Kabukçuoğlu Dur, Kandilov & Leblebicioglu** — *Policy Uncertainty and Foreign Investors' Entry Mode Choice: Greenfield vs. Cross-Border* | SSRN working paper, 2025 | Tests whether policy uncertainty shifts entry from greenfield toward acquisition |
| **Zhou, Kumar, Yu & Jiang (2021)** — *The economic policy uncertainty and the choice of entry mode of outward foreign direct investment* | Journal of Asian Economics, DOI [10.1016/j.asieco.2021.101306](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.asieco.2021.101306) | EPU and greenfield/M&A choice, home-country side |

Kabukçuoğlu et al. is essentially your RQ6, done at the cross-border level.

So RQ6 must be written as **"does the same result hold for India, monthly, at the DPIIT route level?"** — a replication and an extension — not as a new question. If you present it as new, a well-read examiner will find this paper. If you present it as an India-specific test with monthly data and a cleaner decomposition, it is a genuine contribution.

## 5. The gap, stated properly

> Existing evidence that economic policy uncertainty reduces foreign direct investment into India is linear (Sinha and Ghosh, 2021; Khan and Qamar, 2023). It estimates a single elasticity and therefore assumes that increases and decreases in uncertainty have equal and opposite effects. Asymmetric methods have been applied to Indian data, but to total investment (Adil and Roy, 2024) and to the equity market (Simran and Sharma, 2024), neither of which is subject to the irreversibility that makes a factory different from a share. No study decomposes Indian FDI into the positive and negative movements of policy uncertainty, traces the response month by month after a shock, allows the relation to change across policy periods, or separates domestic from global uncertainty while holding the others constant. Entry-mode work on policy uncertainty exists at the cross-border level (Kabukçuoğlu Dur, Kandilov and Leblebicioglu) but has not been run on India's monthly route-wise FDI data.

That paragraph is defensible. The one in your draft is not.
